Chalkhold

A white citadel built in tiers. You come in through a gate between two drum towers, cross the lower ward past the stables and smithy, and climb a grand stair to the upper ward. There the white-paved Court of the White Tree stands before a high-roofed Great Hall, and behind the hall the White Tower rises to a slate spire. The ten bedrooms are in the tower, two to a floor. This compact version uses less than half the chalk of the full-size citadel: chalk goes where the castle shows white, and floors, paving and hidden foundations use ordinary stone and timber. Every block is drawn below, one layer at a time.

The citadel, tier by tier

Players walk on layer 1 in the lower ward and undercroft, and on layer 5 in the upper ward and along every wall-walk. The tower floors are 5 layers apart: the war room on 5, the solar on 10, bedrooms on 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35, and the lookout on 40. One spiral stair in the turret beside the White Tower links the crypt at the bottom to the lookout at the top.

The ten bedrooms

Floors 2 to 6 of the White Tower each have a corridor across the middle with a half-round bedroom on either side. Each room is about 32 blocks of floor, 4 blocks tall, with two arrow-slit windows. Every room has its own colour for the bed, rug and the banner over the bed, and the same colour banner hangs for that player in the Great Hall.

Storage within reach of the benches

Hytale's crafting benches take materials straight from nearby chests. On your server the reach is 14 blocks out from the edge of the bench in every direction, and 7 layers up or down from the bench's own layer. The castle keeps a store of chests inside that range for every bench, and keeps the bedroom chests outside it, so crafting never uses up anyone's personal items.

Workshop. Directly under the workshop, in the undercroft, are three rows of 8 chests, 4 layers below the benches. A ladder hatch in the workshop's south-east corner drops straight down to them. Each bench also has a chest beside it, and the Arcanist's Workbench reaches part of the east vault too.

Kitchen. The Chef's Stove reaches the two rows of chests in the kitchen stores below (down the ladder by the hearth), the chest row next to the stove and the chest row along the pantry.

Smithy. The smithy is level with the undercroft, so all five of its benches reach the east half of the chest vault through the terrace wall, plus the chests in the smithy itself.

Bedrooms are out of reach on purpose. The solar sits on tower floor 1 and the bedrooms start on floor 2 (layer 15), 10 layers above the benches. That's too high for any bench to reach. The west half of the vault is also out of reach of every bench except one corner of the Armorer's, so it suits long-term storage.

Materials

Exact counts from the blueprint. The spare column adds 10% for mistakes and changes of mind. Stair facings and colours are combined into one line each. This version needs about 6,500 chalk blocks, against about 14,900 for the full-size citadel. Most of the saving comes from a smaller site and thinner walls. The rest comes from using grey stone for paving, floors, the undercroft pillars and the tower bases hidden in the undercroft, and timber for interior partitions and the workshop.

Build order

Work bottom-up and tick off each layer under the plan as you finish it. At around 15,000 blocks this is a group project. One crew can take the walls and gatehouse while another builds the tower, as long as both work from the same corner stone.

  1. Pick and flatten a site about 49 × 69 blocks. The plan has north at the top and the main gate at the south. Turn it however suits your land, but keep everything else in the same relative positions.
  2. Mark the corner stone. Put a marker block at col 2, row 2 on layer 0. That's the outer north-west corner of the curtain wall, and every other position is counted from the same grid.
  3. Layer 0: foundations. A grey stone course under every wall and tower, then the paving, the stable and smithy floors, the well and the godswood pool.
  4. Layers 1–3: walls and undercroft. Curtain walls, the terrace front wall with its two undercroft doors, the round towers, the gatehouse and the grand stair. Then the undercroft: stone pillars, chest vault, wine cellar, armoury, hall of statues and crypt. Start the spiral stair here and add its steps on every layer from now on.
  5. Layer 4: the upper ward deck. The biggest single layer: grey paving across the upper ward and white chalk brick across the court. The timber wall-walk goes in on this layer too. Every water, grass and farmland block on this layer needs a paving block under it on layer 3.
  6. Layers 5–15: the upper ward. The Great Hall, the kitchen and pantry, the timber workshop, and the solar and war room. Battlements go on the curtain wall (layers 5–6), and the round towers rise to their cones or battlements.
  7. Layers 16–26: the hall roof. The gabled slate roof, the gable walls with the rose window, and the pinnacles at the front corners.
  8. Layers 10–44: the White Tower. Five bedroom floors, the solar and the lookout, with the spiral turret climbing alongside. The gallery's corbels go on layer 38 and its walkway on layer 39.
  9. Layers 45–62: the spires. The White Tower's spire, the turret's taller cap and the flags.
  10. Furnish. Workstations and storage first so the castle is useful early. Then the bedrooms and the Great Hall, then everything else from the list above.

Reading the plan in Hytale

Arrows on stairs. On Chalk Brick Stairs the arrow points the way you walk up, which is also the tall back side of the step. Hollow arrows are upside-down stairs. They form the arch shoulders, mantels and corbels, and their tall side goes toward the arrow. On roof stairs the arrow points uphill toward the ridge or peak.

Round towers. The curves are drawn block by block. Copy each layer exactly rather than eyeballing the circle and they'll come out clean.

Furniture bigger than one block. Workstations, beds and carts get a reserved space: a labelled cell plus a darker "space" cell next to it. If a Hytale model is bigger, slide it within its room. Nothing structural depends on furniture.

Water on the upper ward. The upper ward is a deck over a hollow undercroft. Every water, grass and farmland block on layer 4 has a paving block under it on layer 3, so nothing drains through.

Fire indoors. The hall fire pit, kitchen hearth and braziers are drawn as fire. If fire spreads on your server, use campfires there instead.

Block names. Chalk comes in Brick, Stairs, Half Slab, Wall, Beam and Pillar variants. Look for the recipes at the Builder's Workbench. Accent blocks are suggestions: any grey stone for the paving and foundations, any dark wood for floors and beams, any slate or blue roof block and its stairs for the roofs, any pale log for the white trees.

Colours. The ten room colours are Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Teal, Sky Blue, Purple, Pink, Brown and Grey. Swap in whatever cloth colours your server has, as long as each room gets its own.
