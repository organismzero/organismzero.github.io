Hytale citadel plan · 10 players · chalk brick

Chalkhold

A white citadel built in tiers. You come in through a gate between two drum towers, cross the lower ward past the stables and smithy, and climb a grand stair to the upper ward. There the white-paved Court of the White Tree stands before a high-roofed Great Hall, and behind the hall the White Tower rises to a tall, steep spire. The ten bedrooms are in the tower, two to a floor. This compact version uses less than half the chalk of the full-size citadel: chalk goes where the castle shows white, and floors, paving and hidden foundations use ordinary stone and timber. Every block is drawn below, one layer at a time.

    The citadel, tier by tier

    Players walk on layer 1 in the lower ward and undercroft, and on layer 5 in the upper ward and along every wall-walk. The tower floors are 5 layers apart: the war room on 5, the solar on 10, bedrooms on 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35, and the lookout on 40. One spiral stair in the turret beside the White Tower links the crypt at the bottom to the lookout at the top.

    The ten bedrooms

    Floors 2 to 6 of the White Tower each have a corridor across the middle with a half-round bedroom on either side. Each room is about 32 blocks of floor, 4 blocks tall, with two windows. Every room has its own colour: a cloth carpet set into the floor and a column of cloth over the bed as its banner. The same colour banner hangs for that player in the Great Hall.

    Storage within reach of the benches

    Hytale's crafting benches take materials straight from nearby chests. On your server the reach is 14 blocks out from the edge of the bench in every direction, and 7 layers up or down from the bench's own layer. The castle keeps a store of chests inside that range for every bench, and keeps the bedroom chests outside it, so crafting never uses up anyone's personal items.

    Workshop. Directly under the workshop, in the undercroft, are three rows of 8 chests, 4 layers below the benches. A ladder hatch in the workshop's south-east corner drops straight down to them. Each bench also has a chest beside it, and the Arcanist's Workbench reaches part of the east vault too.

    Kitchen. The Chef's Stove reaches the two rows of chests in the kitchen stores below (down the ladder by the hearth), the chest row next to the stove and the chest row along the pantry.

    Smithy. The smithy is level with the undercroft, so all five of its benches reach the east half of the chest vault through the terrace wall, plus the chests in the smithy itself.

    Bedrooms are out of reach on purpose. The solar sits on tower floor 1 and the bedrooms start on floor 2 (layer 15), 10 layers above the benches. That's too high for any bench to reach. The west half of the vault is also out of reach of every bench except one corner of the Armorer's, so it suits long-term storage.

    Materials

    Exact counts from the blueprint. The spare column adds 10% for mistakes and changes of mind. Stair facings share one line, and each cloth colour's carpet and banner blocks share one line. Under each name is the block's id in the game, so you can search for it. This version needs about 8,300 chalk blocks, about 1,300 of them roof pieces, against about 14,900 for the full-size citadel. Most of the saving comes from a smaller site and thinner walls. The rest comes from using grey stone for paving, floors, the undercroft pillars and the tower bases hidden in the undercroft, and timber for interior partitions and the workshop.

    Rebuilding from the version you built

    If you built the previous plan, you don't need to start again. Turn on Changes since built version above the plan and it marks every block that differs on each layer: a solid outline where a block changes, a solid outline with a corner flag for a new block, and a dashed outline with a cross for a block to take out. Furniture is quick to move, so the structure count is the one that takes work.

    What changed and why

    • Windows. Windows moved off the corners and the diagonal steps of the round walls. A game window is a thin panel that needs a full block on each side, so some now have a chalk jamb beside them. Each jamb has a Chalk - Stairs on top and an upside-down one underneath, tall side to the wall it leans on, so it reads as a small buttress outside and a neat reveal inside.
    • Pillar stripes and floor bands. The chalk stripes and the band at each tower floor, set into the walls, are now full Chalk Brick - Ornate, which stands out against the smooth walls. The game's pillar and beam blocks are slim and left slits in the wall, so pillars now only stand free: columns, gate posts, the hall pinnacles and the brazier posts.
    • Workshop frame. The timber frame is full cedar logs instead of thin beams, for the same reason.
    • Tables. Every table in the game is 2 × 2, so side tables, nightstands, desks, counters and the feasting tables are decorative plank blocks.
    • Statues. There's no statue block to craft, so each statue is now a chalk column.
    • Big furniture. Workbenches, beds and wardrobes take their real space: most workbenches are 2 tall, beds are 2 wide, 3 long and 2 tall, and wardrobes 2 wide and 2 tall. A few old blocks around them have to go.
    • Bedrooms. Each room's rug is now cloth carpet set into the floor in place of the planks, in the new room colours, and the banner over the bed is a column of the same cloth.
    • Things the game doesn't have. Barrels became chests, grain sacks and carts became hay bales, armchairs became chairs and horse troughs became buckets. The telescopes and raven roosts are gone.

    Build order

    Work bottom-up and tick off each layer under the plan as you finish it. At around 16,000 blocks this is a group project. One crew can take the walls and gatehouse while another builds the tower, as long as both work from the same corner stone.

    1. Pick and flatten a site about 49 × 69 blocks. The plan has north at the top and the main gate at the south. Turn it however suits your land, but keep everything else in the same relative positions.
    2. Mark the corner stone. Put a marker block at col 2, row 2 on layer 0. That's the outer north-west corner of the curtain wall, and every other position is counted from the same grid.
    3. Layer 0: foundations. A grey stone course under every wall and tower, then the paving, the stable and smithy floors, the well and the godswood pool.
    4. Layers 1–3: walls and undercroft. Curtain walls, the terrace front wall with its two undercroft doors, the round towers, the gatehouse and the grand stair. Then the undercroft: stone pillars, chest vault, wine cellar, armoury, hall of chalk columns and crypt. Start the spiral stair here and add its steps on every layer from now on.
    5. Layer 4: the upper ward deck. The biggest single layer: grey paving across the upper ward and white smooth chalk brick across the court. The timber wall-walk goes in on this layer too. Every water, grass and farmland block on this layer needs a paving block under it on layer 3.
    6. Layers 5–15: the upper ward. The Great Hall, the kitchen and pantry, the timber workshop, and the solar and war room. Battlements go on the curtain wall (layers 5–6), and the round towers rise to their spires or battlements.
    7. Layers 16–26: the hall roof. The gabled roof with its Flat cap along the ridge, the gable walls with the triple lancet window, and the pinnacles at the front corners.
    8. Layers 10–44: the White Tower. Five bedroom floors, the solar and the lookout, with the spiral turret climbing alongside. The gallery's corbels go on layer 38 and its walkway on layer 39.
    9. Layers 45–61: the spires. The White Tower's spire, the turret's last two courses and its own spire, and the flags.
    10. Furnish. Workstations and storage first so the castle is useful early. Then the bedrooms and the Great Hall, then everything else from the list above.

    Reading the plan in Hytale

    Arrows on stairs. On Chalk Brick Stairs the arrow points the way you walk up, which is also the tall back side of the step. Hollow arrows are upside-down stairs. They form the arch shoulders, mantels and corbels, and their tall side goes toward the arrow. On roof pieces the arrow points to the tall side, uphill toward the ridge or peak (see Roofs below).

    Roofs. Every roof is made of Chalk Cobble Roof pieces, crafted from raw chalk. The arrow on a roof piece points to its tall side. A single arrow is a Chalk Cobble Roof, which climbs one block over one block. A double arrow is a Chalk Cobble Roof - Steep, which climbs two blocks over one block and is two blocks tall: its top half fills the hatched cell on the layer above, so leave that cell clear. Place each piece the way its arrow points and the game shapes the joins itself. A corner piece turns into a hip corner once the piece beside it on its tall side is down, and a piece with nothing solid under it turns into a thin Hollow piece, which gives the hall and workshop a smooth sloped ceiling. You never place corner or Hollow pieces yourself; if a corner doesn't turn, break it and place it again after its neighbours. The thin cap along the hall's ridge is a Chalk Cobble Roof - Flat, and the piece on top of the well's canopy turns into the small Topper cap.

    Tower spires. Roof pieces only face north, east, south or west, so each round tower carries a square spire: rings of Steep pieces, each one block in and two layers up from the one below, then a ring of plain Chalk Cobble Roof round a chalk block that holds the flag. The bottom ring reaches one block past the wall on each side, and further at the corners, where chalk blocks inside it close the gap over the curve of the wall. The stair turret's spire starts above the White Tower's lowest rings, and its west eave rests on the big spire's slope.

    Round towers. The curves are drawn block by block. Copy each layer exactly rather than eyeballing the circle and they'll come out clean.

    Furniture bigger than one block. Big items are drawn at their size in the game: a labelled cell where you place the item, plus hatched cells for the rest of it, sometimes on the layer above. Workstations are 2 wide and 2 tall (the Workbench is only 1 tall and the Salvager's Workbench only 1 wide). Tavern Beds are 2 wide, 3 long and 2 tall, wardrobes 2 wide and 2 tall, and Tavern Benches 2 wide. Every table in the game is 2 × 2, so the side tables, nightstands, desks, counters and feasting tables are Darkwood Planks - Decorative blocks instead. Nothing structural depends on furniture.

    Doors and windows. These are thin panels, so each one sits between two full blocks. Where a window in a round wall had nothing solid beside it, the plan adds a chalk jamb. Every jamb, outside or in the room, gets a stair on top and an upside-down stair underneath, both with the tall side against the wall it leans on. Doors that open to the outdoors are marble Light Temple Doors to go with the windows, and doors between rooms are darkwood Tavern Doors. The two 3-wide doorways, the Great Hall's south door and the inner gate, each take one Medium Light Temple Door, 3 wide and 3 tall. It can't be crafted, so gather it. Hanging lanterns are the wooden Lantern among timber and the marble Light Temple Lantern among stone and chalk.

    Water on the upper ward. The upper ward is a deck over a hollow undercroft. Every water, grass and farmland block on layer 4 has a paving block under it on layer 3, so nothing drains through.

    Fire indoors. The hall fire pit, kitchen hearth and beacon are Campfires, and the braziers are Light Temple Braziers, so there's no loose fire anywhere in the castle.

    Block names. Every block in the plan is a real block in Hytale 0.7.4, and each row of the materials list shows its exact block id so you can search for it in game. Mine raw chalk and craft everything from it at the Builder's Workbench: Chalk Brick - Smooth for the walls and Chalk Brick - Ornate for the stripes and bands. The Chalk Brick Pillar, Half Slab and Wall recipes ask for chalk brick, and smooth or ornate chalk brick works for that. Each pillar starts with a Pillar - Base, with Pillar - Middle blocks above it; a brazier can stand on a Base but not on a Middle. Textured Chalk Brick only comes from structures in the world, and the Chalk Brick Stairs and Beam can't be crafted, so the plan uses Chalk - Stairs and Chalk - Beam.

    Colours. The ten room colours are Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Cyan, Light Blue, Purple, Pink, Gray and Black, all cloth (wool) colours in the game. Beds only come in one look, so the colour is in each room's carpet and banner.
